# Forensic Methods and Limitations

## ELA

Error Level Analysis can highlight recompression differences, but platform exports, screenshots, resizing, and editing pipelines can create misleading patterns. ELA is a triage artifact, not proof of AI generation or manipulation.

## Compression Fingerprints

JPEG markers, quantization tables, rough quality estimates, and recompression sweeps can support comparisons between copies. They are affected by platforms, editors, messengers, and screenshots. They do not establish authorship or AI generation.

## EXIF

EXIF can support provenance when present, but it is often stripped or rewritten. Missing EXIF is common for social media and messaging apps. Present EXIF can also be incomplete or fabricated.

## Video Metadata

ffprobe metadata, codec/container fields, string scans, extracted frames, corner crops, and frame-difference summaries are useful for review. Video platforms and editing tools routinely rewrite metadata and recompress media.

## Provenance Beats Detector Claims

The strongest evidence usually comes from raw originals, platform originals, source chronology, archive captures, and independent corroboration. A detector-like score without provenance is weak evidence and should not drive conclusions.

## Originals and Chronology

Raw originals and source chronology help answer where media first appeared, whether later copies were recompressed or edited, and what would prove or disprove an allegation.
